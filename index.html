1 Engagement & finding details

RCA is performed after a deficiency is already identified. This tool diagnoses why the deficiency occurred — it is not a tool for detecting whether something was missed (that is the review / EQR function).

Better-practice scope reminder (ASIC REP 739)
ASIC recommends running RCA not only on negative findings but also on: a sample of positive internal-monitoring files (to learn what makes a good audit), all regulator-reviewed files with findings, and listed-entity files with a restatement or ASIC media release.

2 RCA preparer, independence & oversight

ASIC found firms that kept RCA independent of the engagement team and the monitoring team reached better conclusions. The preparer should not be the engagement partner or the person who performed the monitoring review.

Preparer is independent of the engagement team
Not the engagement partner, manager or a member of the engagement team for this file.
Preparer is independent of the monitoring reviewer
Not the person who performed the internal monitoring review that raised the finding.

3 Information gathering — who was interviewed

ASIC's single biggest gap: 0 of 6 firms interviewed all relevant people. Interview everyone who worked on the affected area — including the EQR and specialists. If a relevant person is not interviewed, record why.

Why this matters
Gather information promptly while memories are fresh and before staff move on. Use individual interviews (more candid than group), with a tailored agenda of topics. Review the working papers and the methodology/guidance in force at the time.

4 Audit Quality Indicators (AQIs) & milestones

ASIC noted firms collected AQIs but rarely used them to reach the root cause. Capture the data, then ask whether it correlates with the finding.

5 Root cause analysis — drill down (Five Whys)

Ask "why?" repeatedly — to a maximum of five levels — until you reach a cause you can act on. A finding may have more than one cause chain. Do not stop at a symptom (e.g. "insufficient documentation" is usually a symptom, not the root cause).

Symptom vs root cause — worked example
Finding: cash-flow misclassification not identified. Why? Senior lacked understanding → Why? Inexperienced senior on the area → Why? Booked senior left / resource shortage → Why? Poor project management & fee pressure. Root cause = resourcing + project management, not "lack of training".

6 Root cause classification

Classify each identified root cause against the ASIC taxonomy. Identify whether it is internal (firm can influence) or external.

ASIC root-cause categories (REP 739, Fig 2)
Knowledge & skills of individuals · Professional scepticism & mindset · Supervision & review · Insufficient documentation · Use of specialists & experts · High number of complex issues · Project management & resourcing · Motivation & commitment · Firm policies/procedures not followed.

Watch-outs: "Documentation" and "training need" are the two most common mislabelled root causes — they are usually symptoms of supervision, scepticism, skills or project-management failures.

7 Remediation / action plan

ASIC's main criticism: 10 of 12 RCAs defaulted to "more training". Map an action to each root cause, push beyond training, assign an owner and date, and plan a real-time review on the next cycle to confirm the fix worked.

Wider actions to consider (beyond training)
Supervision & review changes · skill-mix / resourcing on the engagement · methodology, tools & template improvements · project-management & milestone monitoring · real-time review & coaching next cycle · client / audit-committee communication · partner portfolio & workload review · risk profiling for acceptance/continuance · behavioural / cultural actions.

8 Conclusion, themes & sign-off

Better-practice checklist (use before finalising)
  • RCA was timely — done while matters were fresh
  • All relevant team members, EQR and specialists interviewed (or omission justified)
  • Symptom vs root cause challenged — drilled down, not a quick fix
  • AQIs/milestone data actually used to reach the conclusion
  • Preparer independent of engagement and monitoring teams
  • Actions go beyond training; each root cause has an owner and date
  • Real-time review/coaching planned for the next cycle
  • No blame culture — focus on system, not the individual
